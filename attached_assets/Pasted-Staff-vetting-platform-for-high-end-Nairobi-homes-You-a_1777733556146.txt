Staff vetting platform for high-end Nairobi homes
You are building a full production-grade domestic staff vetting and background verification platform for the Kenyan high-end residential market. Before writing any code, read this entire brief, think deeply, expand the vision significantly with your best ideas, then present a phased plan of approximately 20 substantial phases. Wait for "next" before executing anything.
The pain:
Families in Karen, Runda, Muthaiga, Kitisuru, and Gigiri hire housekeepers, nannies, cooks, drivers, gardeners, and security personnel constantly. The process is entirely trust-and-referral based — you ask a neighbour, you call a relative, you post in a WhatsApp group. The risk is enormous. Theft, child safety incidents, and fraud are common. The Certificate of Good Conduct from the DCI takes 3–6 weeks, is easily forged, and tells you nothing about employment history or references. There is no structured, fast, trustworthy vetting service designed for this market. The families who need it most — expats, high-net-worth Kenyans, dual-income households — have money and genuine fear and would pay well for something credible.
What you are building:
A platform where a homeowner submits a domestic worker's details and within 24–48 hours receives a clean, professional vetting report. The report covers: identity verification (national ID cross-check), reference calls to previous employers (human-conducted, recorded, summarised by AI), DCI certificate status check, social media review, and an overall trust score. The platform also builds a growing database of vetted workers — over time, a vetted worker gets a digital profile and a QR-code "verified" card they can show future employers. Employers search the database before hiring. Workers are incentivised to get vetted because it makes them more hireable.
Critical nuances:
The Kenyan DCI Good Conduct certificate can be applied for online at https://www.dci.go.ke — the platform should guide users through this or offer to handle the application on behalf of the worker as a paid add-on. Reference verification must be human-conducted — automated calls won't work in Kenya — but the platform should provide structured call scripts, recording tools, and AI summarisation of the call. National ID verification should use available APIs (e.g. the IPRS system where accessible, or structured manual cross-check). The trust score must be transparent — not a black box — so families understand exactly what was checked and what wasn't. There should be a tiered product: a basic Ksh 2,500 check, a standard Ksh 5,000 check, and a premium Ksh 9,000 deep check including physical address visit. The worker's digital verified profile should be portable — they own it, and can share it with any future employer. This is the ethical spine of the product and will drive word-of-mouth from workers themselves.
Expand on this vision. Consider: a repeat-employer dashboard where families manage all their current staff records in one place; annual re-vetting reminders; an incident reporting feature (private, legal-review before any action); a network trust layer where a worker vetted by three Karen households carries a social trust signal; integration with the Karen Residents Association, Runda Residents Association, and similar groups as distribution channels; a corporate version for office cleaning companies and hospitality businesses; and anything else that elevates this into the definitive trust infrastructure for domestic employment in Kenya.

Background job queue for vetting workflow steps. Proper admin dashboard for the operations team managing the human verification steps.
